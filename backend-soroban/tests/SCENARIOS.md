# Scenario catalogue

Every behaviour the system must show, with the tests that run it (TESTING.md, "Scenario catalogue"). Scenarios are numbered by the model action they exercise (`../../stellar-research/definition/rtgs-definition.md` §4.6), so a family with no rows is an action with no test.

- Deposit and withdraw are A11 (definition §5.5): a transfer between a bank's trustline holding of the cash token and its settlement balance in the contract.
- `storage::` rows (SC-ST) are about the ledger, not the model: storage lifetime and transaction cost.
- Test paths are relative to the `settlement` crate: `unit::…` stands for `tests::unit::…`, and `auth::…` for `tests::auth::…`.
- `core::…` stands for `rtgs_core::tests::…`, and `core::props::…` for `rtgs_core::props::…`.
- `integration/…` rows (SC-IT, SC-H) run on the local devnet (TESTING.md §4). They cover what the test host can't show: issuer flags, trustlines, real signatures, RPC and on-chain TTL.
- `itf_replay::…` stands for `tests::itf_replay::…`, and `qnt::…` for a `run` in `quint/settlement_test.qnt`.
- `props::…`, `state_machine::…` and `reference::…` stand for `tests::props::…`, `tests::state_machine::…` and `tests::reference::…`.
- The SC-P rows are listed only in their own section, "Properties over many inputs", not in the Test column of the action rows whose behaviour they also run.

**Status, 2026-10-25.** The tests are written. The constructor, `deposit` with its guards, `withdraw`, `submit`, `revoke`, `reprioritise`, `expire`, `set_collateral`, `open_repo`, `repay`, `convert_overdue`, the `balance`, `payment`, `queue`, `queued_count`, `credit`, `repo`, `config` and `central_bank` views, the kernel's `covers`, `greatest_feasible_prefix` and `balances_after`, and the LSM offset have code, and the eager closure at the queue cap fits Mainnet's per-transaction limits. `submit` settles a covered payment at once unless the sender has a payment queued ahead of it (S7), and queues any other; when the queue was already full, it refuses a payment still queued after the eager closure (ADR-0010). The model-based replay passes. After a deposit, a submission that settles at once, a revocation, a reprioritisation or an expiry, the eager closure settles every covered head and follows the cascade (S9). `revoke` takes a queued payment out of its queue (A6, ADR-0016), and `reprioritise` moves one to the other priority at its place by age (A8, ADR-0009). `expire` lets anyone reject a queued payment past its expiry (A5, ADR-0015). `set_collateral` lets the central bank alone set a bank's collateral, 0 or more and never below the credit it backs (A14, S21, ADR-0011). `open_repo` lets the central bank alone open a repo up to a bank's collateral, minting it into the contract and crediting the bank, then runs the eager closure (A12, ADR-0011). `repay` lets the bank, or the central bank on its behalf, repay a repo, burning the repayment from the contract (A13, ADR-0011), and lowering `ovn` instead of `cr` for a penalty repo (A20). `convert_overdue` lets anyone turn a repo past its term into a penalty repo, moving its outstanding amount from `cr` to `ovn` and recording the penalty rate (ADR-0014). Once gross settlement is at rest, after every such call and after a submission that queues, the contract offsets the greatest feasible prefix of every queued bank's payments, booking each bank's net change exactly (A3, ADR-0010). The tests that exercise only this code pass: SC-A11-01 to SC-A11-16, SC-A05-01 to SC-A05-10, SC-A06-01 to SC-A06-10, SC-A08-01 to SC-A08-11, SC-A14-01 to SC-A14-04, SC-A12-01 to SC-A12-08, SC-A13-01 to SC-A13-10, SC-A20-01 to SC-A20-12, SC-A01-01 to SC-A01-24, SC-A02-01 to SC-A02-09, SC-A03-01 to SC-A03-23, SC-P-03 to SC-P-08, SC-P-10 to SC-P-15, SC-ST-01, SC-ST-02 and SC-ST-04 to SC-ST-23, SC-MB-01, and on the devnet SC-IT-01 to SC-IT-06. SC-P-09 passes too: it checks the reference rule the property tests are judged by. So does SC-MB-02. SC-ST-03 checks, since Flow 20, that each entry point on a ledger of its own extends the instance and every entry it writes, the eager closure's rewrites included. SC-P-01 and SC-P-02, which call every entry point, pass as well, and they catch hand mutants of the credit code's edges (Flow 21). Every entry point has code, and every unit, auth, property and replay test passes. Only the devnet scenario SC-H-02 stays pending, and red, until an issuance controller holds the SAC admin role.

## A15 `Mint`, A16 `Burn`

Covered through the SAC. The issuer's mint happens inside `open_repo` (SC-A12-01, SC-A12-07), and the contract's burn inside `repay` (SC-A13-01, SC-A20-07). Deposit and withdraw move existing money between a bank's two accounts (A11, SC-A11), so they are neither. Clawback never undoes a final payment (ADR-0006). It is an issuer power outside the contract, and the integration layer tests it.

## A17 `Open`, A18 `Close`

Not applicable: there is no business day (ADR-0013). A18's rejection of unsettled payments becomes expiry (A5, ADR-0015). Its conversion of unpaid credit becomes the per-repo penalty step (below, ADR-0014).

## A19 `Tick`

This is the ledger's close time, set in tests with `env.ledger().set_timestamp`. The expiry and term tests (SC-A05, SC-A20) use it.

## Properties over many inputs

The property tests check a rule over generated inputs rather than one example (TESTING.md §2). CI runs them with a pinned seed; set `PROPTEST_RNG_SEED` and `PROPTEST_CASES` to explore further.

The state machine `state_machine::the_contract_refines_the_reference_model` replays random sequences of every entry point against a reference model of §4 under the ADRs, comparing each outcome and the whole state. It covers the state changes and error outcomes of A1, A2, A3, A5, A6, A8, A11 to A14, A19 and A20, the actions of SC-P-02's Model IDs, for positive amounts of 1 to 300, the exact or one-over holdings, balances and collateral headroom, the exact repo amounts, payments past their expiry, and repos past their term or exactly at it. It also aims some calls: at penalty repos, at repaid ones, at a bank owing on two repos, at collateral exactly at the credit it backs, and at a repayment over both the outstanding amount and the balance. It repays half, or all but one, of what a repo owes, so a partly repaid repo is converted and a repo left 1 short is not `Repaid`. At the pinned seed and seeds 1 to 4, it kills the hand mutants listed in StillToFix.md, Flow 21, of the guards, boundaries and bookings of positive amounts in `open_repo`, `repay`, `convert_overdue` and `set_collateral`. Zero or negative repo and repayment amounts and negative collateral are not covered, and neither is a mint called without `try_`: amounts of 300 at most never overflow. SC-P-01 and the unit tests cover both. It does not cover:
- events;
- authorization: every auth is mocked, so the `auth::` tests alone cover S6;
- zero, negative or `i128`-extreme amounts, which SC-P-01 sends;
- the queue cap, which SC-P-01 and SC-P-11 cover;
- deauthorized holders (SC-A11-15, SC-A11-16).

**Expiry as `quint/settlement.qnt` has it (ADR-0015, provisional).** Only `expire` expires a payment. A payment past its expiry stays queued, keeps blocking its queue, and can still settle or be revoked until `expire` is called. The Quint model states this, and its replay (SC-MB-01) already pins it; the model of SC-P-02 and SC-P-01's at-rest check assume it too. ADR-0015 also lets any call that meets an expired head reject it, and S12's τ_rej would forbid settling after the expiry. Whether v1 should do either is still a decision for the user (README). If it should, change `settlement.qnt`, the state machine's `settle` and `expire`, and SC-P-01's at-rest check together.

**Two requirements SC-P-01 states.** An offset books each bank's net change, computed exactly, for example with the 64-bit split of `reference::net_is_non_negative`. By S1 every end balance fits in `i128`, but neither booking payment by payment nor applying every debit first stays in range: both overflow at the `i128` limit. SC-A03-11 and SC-A03-12 pin this too. A refused SAC call, such as an `open_repo` mint that would overflow the contract's holding, comes back as a contract error, not a host abort.

**One requirement SC-P-02 states in full.** Where an input breaks several guards at once, the error is the first of: unknown id, then final status, then the specific guard. `quint/settlement.qnt`, and so SC-MB-01, already fix this order for `reprioritise` (`UnknownPayment`, `NotQueued`, `SamePriority`) and `expire` (`UnknownPayment`, `NotQueued`, `NotExpired`). SC-P-02 adds `repay`'s: `UnknownRepo`, `NotAuthorized`, `RepayExceedsRepo`, `InsufficientFunds`. An amount of 0 or less comes before all of them, as `InvalidAmount`, as in `deposit`, `submit` and `open_repo`; SC-P-02 draws only positive amounts, so it does not pin this. For `reprioritise`, SC-A08-09 and SC-P-14 already pin the order, and for `expire`, SC-P-15; `repay`'s is left to SC-P-02.
